# E!nkster 397 v0.3.0: the archives and the battery log
27 October 2026. Julien's ship of 27.10: "the reader before the language release, and ship", plus "we need a battery drain log like the 154". After v0.2.1 (the typewriter, Enter draws the page clean). `ek_shared.h`, `fonts.h` and the packs are unchanged.

**What ships:**
- **Archives:** Kiwix archives, turned into plain text on the MegaBrain, are read on the 397 from the microSD. With the keyboard, you type a title; without it, the dial picks a first letter.
- **Battery log:** a sample every 30 minutes, as on the 154, with a battery page under Device and a table to download.
- **Charge state:** the 397 now tells "charging", "topping up" and "full" apart.

## 1. On the MegaBrain: turn an archive into text (Windows)
You do this once per archive. The converter is `shelf_convert.py`, a single Python file.

1. **Python.** Install Python 3.12 or newer from python.org and tick **"Add python.exe to PATH"** in the installer.
   - Kiwix's library has ready-made Windows packages for Python 3.10 to 3.14 (checked).
2. **Kiwix's library.** Open PowerShell and type: `py -m pip install libzim`
3. **Put the converter somewhere,** for example `C:\shelf\shelf_convert.py`.
4. **Convert to the SSD first,** not straight onto the card (much faster). For each archive:
   ```
   cd C:\shelf
   py shelf_convert.py "D:\Downloads\wikipedia_en_all_mini_2026-09.zim" C:\shelf\out
   py shelf_convert.py "D:\Downloads\wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi_2026-04.zim" C:\shelf\out
   py shelf_convert.py "D:\Downloads\wikipedia_en_computer_maxi_2026-09.zim" C:\shelf\out
   ```
   (Adjust the folder to wherever your downloads are.)
   - Every 5 seconds it prints how far it is, and roughly how many minutes are left.
   - It uses all the processor's cores but one.
   - At the end it reports what it wrote and what it left out.
5. **Copy to the card.** Copy the folder `C:\shelf\out\shelf` to the root of the microSD, so the card holds `shelf\wikipedia_en_all_mini_2026-09\` and so on.

**The card** must be **FAT32**.
- Most cards up to 32 GB come that way.
- Larger cards come as exFAT. Windows' own format tool won't make FAT32 above 32 GB; a free tool such as Rufus ("Large FAT32") can (not checked).
- The 397 can't read exFAT: it is switched off in the board software (checked).

**How big, how long** (all estimates, not measured on the MegaBrain):
- **Size:** my estimate is about 10 GB of text for the English "mini" archive. That's an intro for each of several million articles, plus the title index. That is **less shrinkage than I suggested yesterday**: I said the text would come out far smaller than the download, and for the mini it won't. The medicine and computer archives lose their images and should come to a few hundred MB together. A 32 GB card should hold all three. The converter prints the real sizes.
- **Time:** here, one processor core converts about 400 short articles a second (measured on a test archive). On the MegaBrain's cores, the mini archive should take somewhere between 20 minutes and an hour.
- **Memory:** the title index is sorted in memory, which takes a few GB of RAM for the mini archive.
- **Optional:** `--no-redirects` leaves out the other names of articles ("USA" for United States). The index is then smaller, but those names can't be searched.

**What the converter does:**
- Keeps paragraphs, headings and list items.
- Drops images, tables, references and links.
- Letters the 397's typeface can't draw are folded to the plain letter where possible (ł to l, ő to o). The rest (Chinese, Arabic, Cyrillic…) show as a small box, and the report counts them.
- Each archive keeps its own licence, written into its folder and shown on every article (Wikipedia: CC BY-SA 4.0).

## 2. Flash
- `einkster397-v0_3_0-nokeys-update.bin` at **0x10000**. It keeps your networks, clock, log and keyboard pairing.
- If anything looks wrong: the `-full.bin` at **0x0**. It erases everything.

## 3. Read
- **Find the tile:** turn the dial to **Archives**, the 5th dot (between Typewriter and Catalog), and press. With more than one archive on the card, a list comes first.
- **With the keyboard:**
  - Press a key so it comes back; the top right says "listening for the keyboard" while it waits.
  - Type the start of a title. The list follows each key.
  | Key | What it does |
  |---|---|
  | ↑ / ↓ | move in the list |
  | Page Up / Page Down | move a whole page of titles |
  | Enter | read the chosen article |
  | Tab | the next archive, with the same search |
  | Esc | back to the tiles |
  - Upper and lower case and accents don't matter: "grusse" finds Grüße.
- **Without a keyboard:** turn the dial to choose a first letter, then press to get the titles. Turn to move, press to read. A long press takes you back to the letter.
- **Reading:**
  | Control | What it does |
  |---|---|
  | Dial | turns the page |
  | Space, Page Down, ↓ or → | next page |
  | Page Up, ↑ or ← | previous page |
  | Home / End | first / last page |
  | Esc, Backspace, or a dial press | back to the titles |
  - The first page shows where the article comes from and its licence.
- **Sleep:**
  - Without the keyboard, the 397 sleeps after 45 s on the page; the dial wakes it and turns at once.
  - With the keyboard, it stays awake 10 minutes.
- **Refreshes:**
  - Every page turn is a clean full refresh: it flashes, but leaves no grey.
  - While you type a search, the list updates with quick partial refreshes, and the page redraws clean after 2 s without a key.
  - If you'd rather have quicker page turns and accept some grey, that is one number to change.
- **Privacy:** the field log says only "archive opened" or "article opened", never which one or what was searched.

## 4. The battery log
- **Every 30 minutes,** asleep or awake (typing and reading included), a sample goes into the log. It holds:
  - the voltage and percentage;
  - USB and the charge state;
  - the room's temperature and humidity;
  - the outside temperature when the forecast is fresh;
  - the Wi-Fi visits;
  - whether the keyboard was connected.

  A sample needs the clock to be set.
- **Device > menu > "Battery: the 30-minute log"** shows:
  - the last 7 days as dots, from 3.3 to 4.25 V, with a mark under each USB sample;
  - the drain since the last charge in %/day, and roughly how long until empty (it needs 6 hours of samples);
  - how long the last top-up took.
- **The table:** Set up > "30-minute log" downloads it as a CSV file. The columns are the same as the 154's, except the fifth, which holds the 397's battery percentage (the 154 counts asks there).
- **Charge state:** the Device tile's Power row and the field log now say "USB, charging", "USB, topping up" or "USB, full".
  - Your 4.20 V at 100 % while still charging was the top-up: the charger holds 4.20 V while the current falls, and stops at 25 mA.
  - The battery page will show how long that takes on your cell (to within 30 minutes).

## What was checked
- **The board build:** it compiles with the GitHub build's compiler (ESP32 core 3.2.0), with no warning in our files (checked).
  - Firmware: 2,119,070 bytes with no keys, in a 4 MB slot.
  - Globals: 160,868 bytes. The state kept through sleep: 3,880 of 8,192 bytes (checked).
- **The full image:** the app at 0x10000 is the update file byte for byte (checked).
- **The bench: 117 checks, 0 failed.** The archive checks run on two archives made by the converter from a test Kiwix archive (3,007 articles, one of them split over three text files):
  - Navigation: the tile; the archive list, which leaves out an archive from another converter version; the letter wheel; B finds Berlin first; L shows "Lodz → Lódz" (another name pointing to its article).
  - Reading: upright, inside the margins, on pages; sleep and wake on a page, with the article read again from the card; turning past the end stays on the last page.
  - The keyboard: it comes back by itself; "grü" finds Grüße; "item 2" finds Item 2000 first among 3,000 titles; Page Down; nothing for "zzz"; Tab to the other archive, where "usa" opens United States from its third file.
  - The clean page after typing pauses, and no card.
  - Privacy: no title or archive name in the log.
  - **Battery:** a sample every 30 minutes, with the first one marked as a power-on start; charging, topping up and full each logged; the battery page; a CSV line; a sample taken while awake with the keyboard.
- **The converter:** run here on the test archive. Every index entry points to the right article in each of the three files, and the output is the same with 1 or 2 cores (checked).
- **Not checked on the board:**
  - the card read speed;
  - search time on a very large index;
  - the converter on your three real archives and on Windows;
  - the charger's states as the power chip reports them.

## Test plan
1. **Flash** the update. Device tile: the Power row says "USB, charging" or "USB, topping up". Leave it on USB until it says "USB, full", then note the time.
2. **Convert** the computer archive first (the smallest) and copy `shelf` onto the card. Archives tile > press: the archive opens.
3. **Without the keyboard:** pick a letter with the dial, open an article, turn a few pages. Photo of the first page (the credit line) and of a page in the middle.
4. **With the keyboard:** type a title you know. How long does the list take to follow a key? A photo of the list, and of any grey after a pause.
5. **Then the big one:** convert the mini archive, then search it. Is the list still quick?
6. **After a day:** Set up > 30-minute log > download, and send it with the field log.
