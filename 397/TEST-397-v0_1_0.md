# E!nkster 397 v0.1.0: first light
26 October 2026. Julien's ship on 26.10: "mine, ship". Your own 397, built like the Classic. Shares `ek_shared.h` 1.0.3, `fonts.h` and the nine packs with the 154 (v0.26.0).

## What it does
The 397 has no touch screen, so the **dial** on the left edge does everything. You read it upright, with the dial on the left.

| Dial | On a tile | In a list | In a card |
|---|---|---|---|
| up / down | previous / next tile | move | page or card back / forward |
| press | open | choose | show the answer (questions), else back to the list |
| long press (0.7 s) | back to the Clock | back one level | back to the list |

**PWR held about 1.5 s** (the power chip's default, not checked) switches it off cleanly ("OFF" on the paper). Held 4 s, the power chip cuts the power on its own. A short press switches it on.

Tiles:
1. **Clock.** Large digits, the date, the weather now and today's low and high, the room's temperature and humidity. Asleep, it wakes every minute and changes only what moved, without flashing. A clean full refresh comes once an hour.
2. **Climate.** Room (SHTC3), outside now, and three days from Open-Meteo. Press: fetch the weather now.
3. **Editions.** The nine packs the 154 carries, cut from the same source at build time, so both boards always hold the same text: Atlantid!a, Vulcan!a, Yog!a, C@tlas, Guineapedi@, W@terfacts! (EN/DE/FR, in your language), Edit!on Zero. Shelf, then sections, then cards. Questions keep their answer until you press. Checklists, codes and **bold** are shown as on the 154.
4. **Catalog.** Your business card (from the build secret) and the InvisibleChaos stations, each as a big QR code.
5. **Device.** Version, battery, power, networks, clock, language. Press for the menu:
   - **Set up** opens a hotspot with its own password and a QR code to join it. The page handles the Wi-Fi networks, the clock from your phone, English or French, and the field log (read or download).
   - Language
   - About and credits
   - Switch off

In the background:
- Every 30 minutes, a battery and room line goes into the field log.
- Every hour, with a known network: the time from the internet and the weather.
- An empty cell (under 3 % and 3.45 V) puts "battery empty" on the paper and switches it off.

## Files
**To upload to the repository**, in a new folder `397/` at the root, next to `einkster154.ino`:
- `397/einkster397.ino`
- `397/prepare397.py`
- `397/partitions.csv`
- `.github/workflows/build.yml`: replace the old one. It adds the 397 to the same build. It reuses your existing secrets (EK_WIFI_SSID, EK_WIFI_PASS, EK_CARD). It fetches only Waveshare's power chip folder.

Optional:
- `397/TEST-397-v0_1_0.md`
- `397/tests/host/` (the bench)

The next push builds `einkster397-v0_1_0-full.bin` and `-update.bin` into the same "latest" release as the 154.

**To flash right now, without waiting for GitHub:** `einkster397-v0_1_0-nokeys-full.bin`. It is the same firmware with no network and no business card inside. You add your Wi-Fi on the setup page. It is safe to keep anywhere.

## Test plan
Waveshare's Chinese demo is replaced. The way back: their full image (`Firmware/Factory procedure/260314.bin` in their repository) at 0x0.

1. **Flash** a full file (`-nokeys-full.bin`, or the Actions one) at **0x0**. It is 16 MB, so it takes a while. If the flashing tool does not find the board: hold BOOT, plug in USB, then release.
2. **Start:** "E!NKSTER / 397 / v0.1.0", then the Clock. If the picture is upside down or mirrored, take a photo and send it: that is one line to change.
3. **Dial:** turn down and up through the five tiles, then press on Editions and read a few cards. Hold the press to go back.
4. **Sleep:** leave it 45 s. Turn the dial: it wakes on the turn and moves at once. On the Clock, watch a minute change: no flash. If the old minute leaves a ghost, say so.
5. **Set up** (no-keys file): Device > press > Set up. Join the hotspot with the code, add your Wi-Fi, press "Set the clock from this phone", then close. Within the hour the weather appears.
6. **Switch off:** hold PWR 1.5 s, and the paper says OFF. Switch on with a short press.
7. **The log:** after a day, Set up > Field log > Download, then send it to me. It holds the drain (a battery line every 30 minutes) and every wake.

## What was checked
- **The firmware compiles** with the GitHub build's compiler: ESP32 Arduino core 3.2.0, ESP32-S3, 16 MB flash, OPI PSRAM. No warning in our file (checked).
  - Firmware: 1,467,626 bytes (bench secrets) and 1,466,826 bytes (no keys), in a 4 MB slot.
  - Globals: 133,740 bytes of RAM. The state kept through sleep takes 3.1 KB of the 8 KB RTC memory.
- **Partition table** (checked in the built file):
  - two 4 MB firmware slots, the update file going at 0x10000;
  - 7.9 MB for the field log and files later.

  The full file is blank over the settings area, so a full flash starts the board as new.
- **The no-keys file holds no network name or password** (checked: searched the binary).
- **The bench:** the sketch itself, run on a computer with the board faked under it. One process per wake, as on the board. 68 checks in 242 wakes, none failed. They cover:
  - **Power on:** two clean refreshes (the name, then the Clock). The two networks from the secrets move into the settings once. The field log opens a run, with no network name or password in it.
  - **The Clock:**
    - asleep, a partial refresh every minute and a clean refresh at each full hour (3 in 3 hours);
    - a battery and room line every 30 minutes;
    - weather older than 3 hours stops showing.
  - **The paper:** every partial refresh starts from exactly what the paper shows, even after a sleep. The board redraws the old picture from the state it keeps through sleep, because the controller needs both pictures. This is the check that matters most for a clean partial refresh.
  - **The dial:** the turn that wakes it counts once.
    - Tiles in both directions.
    - Shelf, sections, cards and pages, then back out with long presses.
    - Inside a card it sleeps until the next half hour and redraws nothing. Woken an hour later, it reads the pack again and turns the page.
  - **Cards:** a question without its answer, then with it; a checklist; a card with a QR code.
  - **Catalog:** the business card first, then each station's code.
  - **Language:** French switched on from the menu, stored as `lang` and logged. Clock, Device and Climate in French.
  - **Other screens:** About; switching off ("OFF" on the paper and a clean end in the log); the setup screen.
  - **Edge cases:**
    - no clock: it says so and wakes every 30 minutes, not every minute;
    - empty battery: "battery empty" and off;
    - USB without a cell: it runs and shows USB.
  - Every screen keeps 6 px clear at the paper's edges.

## Not checked (nothing has run on the board yet)
- **The picture's orientation.** It follows Waveshare's own "270" mapping and the same controller set-up. The direction itself has not been seen on the paper.
- **The partial refresh method:** reset, write both pictures, refresh in mode 0xFF. The command bytes are Waveshare's, and writing the old picture explicitly is my choice. Ghosting over a day: not seen.
- **The dial while asleep:** waking on GPIO 4, 5 and 6 with the internal pull-ups held through sleep. The pins come from Waveshare's code.
- **The power chip:**
  - the settings, copied from Waveshare's demo: charge current 200 mA, cut-off at 2.6 V, PWR 4 s off and 1 s on;
  - the battery percentage;
  - catching the PWR long press;
  - switching off while on USB.
- **Deep sleep current, and so the battery life.** The field log will tell.
- **The clock chip's first reading.** Waveshare's demo may have stored local time, which would make the first reading an hour off. With your networks, the first start sets the clock from the internet. With the no-keys file, use "Set the clock from this phone".
- **Wi-Fi, time, weather and the setup page** on this board. The code is the 154's, reshaped; it compiles but has not run.
